SECTION 1 / 6 · CONCEPT

Acceleration and deceleration

How quickly is the velocity changing?
Definition
Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
  • Symbol: a   Unit: m/s²
  • Velocity rising → +a
  • Velocity falling → −a (deceleration)
  • Acceleration is a vector — direction matters.

Examples:

  • Car speeding up → +a
  • Car braking → −a
  • Ball going up → −a (decelerating)
  • Ball falling back → +a (accelerating)
Formula
a = (v − u) / t
u = initial v,  v = final v.

SECTION 2 / 6 · CONCEPT

Velocity and displacement — starts from rest

A particle moves with uniform acceleration a, starting at rest (u = 0).
Setup
Uniform acceleration, starting from rest
  • Particle starts at rest at t = 0 (u = 0)
  • Acceleration a is constant throughout

Velocity:
v = a·t  (from a = v / t)

Distance:
d = average speed × time
d = (0 + v)/2 × t
d = ½ · a · t²

Velocity
v = a · t
Displacement
d = ½ · a · t²
v–t : line through origin
d–t : upward parabola

Velocity and displacement — non-zero initial velocity

Same particle, but it already has some velocity u when the clock starts.
Setup
Uniform acceleration, initial velocity u
  • At t = 0, particle is moving at velocity u
  • Acceleration a stays constant

v = u + a·t

Distance:
d = u·t + ½·a·t²

  • v–t slope = a, intercept = u

SECTION 4 / 6 · CONCEPT

Deceleration — negative acceleration

When a is negative, the particle slows down. Eventually v reaches zero.
Key Idea
Deceleration is just negative acceleration
  • Initial velocity u
  • Acceleration −a (where a > 0)
  • Each second, lose a m/s of speed
  • Formulas don't change — just plug in a negative a

Velocity: v = u − a·t

Distance: d = u·t − ½·a·t²

Stops when v = 0 ⇒ tstop = u/a

Velocity
v = u − a · t
Displacement
d = u·t − ½·a·t²
v–t : drops to zero
d–t : rises, levels off
SECTION 5 / 6 · CONCEPT

Kinetic friction — a built-in deceleration

Friction between two sliding surfaces opposes motion. It produces a constant deceleration.
Key Idea
Friction = constant deceleration
  • Push a block on a rough floor, then let go.
  • Kinetic friction acts opposite to motion:
    f = μk · N
  • Constant force ⇒ constant deceleration:
    a = μk · g
    (on a horizontal surface)

The block slows at a constant rate until it stops. After stopping, static friction holds it in place — v stays at 0.

Same as §4, but with the deceleration set by μk.

SECTION 6 / 6 · CONCEPT

Slope of one graph = the next graph

Velocity is the slope of d–t. Acceleration is the slope of v–t.
d–t : slope grows
v–t : line, slope = a
►
v–t : slope constant
a–t : horizontal
Key Idea
Read motion off any one graph
  • Slope of d–t at any moment = v
  • Slope of v–t at any moment = a
  • Steep d–t ⇒ large v  |  Flat d–t ⇒ v ≈ 0
  • d–t bending up ⇒ +a  |  d–t bending down ⇒ −a
