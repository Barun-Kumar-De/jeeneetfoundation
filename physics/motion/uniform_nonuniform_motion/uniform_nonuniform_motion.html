Uniform vs Non-uniform Motion

SECTION 1 / 19

Concept — what makes motion 'uniform'?

UNIFORM MOTION
Equal distance in equal intervals of time.
  • Speed stays constant.
  • The distance-time graph is a straight line.
  • Example: a car on cruise control on a flat road.
NON-UNIFORM MOTION
Unequal distances in equal intervals.
  • Speed changes — even if direction does not.
  • The distance-time graph is a curve.
  • Example: a car in city traffic, a falling stone.
THE TEST  —  read it off the data table
If Δs is the same for every equal Δt → uniform.   Otherwise → non-uniform.
t = 0 → 1 sΔs = 5 m t = 1 → 2 sΔs = 5 m t = 2 → 3 sΔs = 5 m
→ uniform: every Δs equals 5 m for every 1-s interval.

JEE BRIDGE  ·  from average speed to instantaneous speed
AVERAGE SPEED OVER AN INTERVAL
v_avg  =  Δs / Δt

Δs = change in distance over the interval Δt. This is the slope of a chord (blue line) between two points on the curve.

NOW SHRINK Δt TOWARDS ZERO

ds = Δs  and  dt = Δt  when Δt is vanishingly small.

v(t)  =  lim (Δt → 0) Δs/Δt  =  ds/dt

SECTION 4 / 19

Instantaneous vs average speed

THE TWO SPEEDS
Average speed
v_avg = Δs / Δt = (s₂ − s₁) / (t₂ − t₁)
Instantaneous speed
v_inst = lim (Δt → 0) Δs / Δt  =  ds / dt

Average is a chord; instantaneous is a tangent. JEE problems use both.

SECTION 6 / 19

MCQ 1 — explanation

CORRECT  ·  Option (b)
Uniform: B  ·  Non-uniform: A, C
Δd PER 30-MIN INTERVAL
IntervalCar ACar BCar C
1:00 → 1:30+2+2+1
1:30 → 2:00+3+2+2
2:00 → 2:30+2+2+1

Car B: Δd = +2 km every interval → equal Δd in equal Δt → uniform.

THE REASONING
  1. Compute Δd between consecutive rows for each car.
  2. Car A: +2, +3, +2. Not constant → non-uniform.
  3. Car B: +2, +2, +2. Constant → uniform.
  4. Car C: +1, +2, +1. Not constant → non-uniform.
COMMON TRAP

Don't confuse 'always moving forward' with 'uniform'. Cars A and C kept moving — but their Δd was unequal between intervals. Uniform motion needs equal Δd in equal Δt, not just non-zero motion.

MCQ 2 — round-trip average speed

Q2. A car covers 3 km from home to school in 10 minutes. On the return trip, it covers the same 3 km in 15 minutes. What is the average speed for the entire journey?

SECTION 8 / 19

MCQ 2 — explanation & the harmonic shortcut

CORRECT  ·  Option (b)
Average speed = 4 m/s
STEP-BY-STEP
  1. 1
    Total distance = 3 + 3 = 6 km = 6000 m
  2. 2
    Total time = 10 + 15 = 25 min = 1500 s
  3. 3
    v_avg = total distance ÷ total time
  4. 4
    v_avg = 6000 / 1500 = 4 m/s
JEE SHORTCUT  ·  EQUAL DISTANCES
v_avg  =  2·v₁·v₂ / (v₁ + v₂)

When the same distance is travelled at two speeds, the average speed is the harmonic mean of the two speeds — never the arithmetic mean.

Check with our values:
v₁ = 3000/600 = 5 m/s    v₂ = 3000/900 ≈ 3.33 m/s
v_avg = 2(5)(3.33) / 8.33 ≈ 4 m/s  ✓

(Arithmetic mean would give (5 + 3.33)/2 ≈ 4.17 m/s — wrong.)

CONCEPT CALLBACK

Average speed is the chord on the d-t graph — it cares only about endpoints, not the path in between.

SCENARIO · WITH STEPS

Same motion — now numbered 1 → 2 → 3.

CORRECT ANSWER

Option C · decaying sawtooth

1
First fall
Released from rest, gravity pulls the ball down. Speed climbs linearly until it hits the floor — that first peak is the tallest.
2
First bounce
Impact flips the velocity. Some energy is lost, so the rebound speed is lower than the impact speed — the cycle starts again from a smaller peak.
3
Decaying peaks
Each bounce loses a fraction of kinetic energy. Peaks shrink and cycles shorten — the classic sawtooth decay.

Through the bottom

Other extreme
The bob momentarily stops at the far side. Speed drops to zero, and the cycle repeats. Speed never goes below the t-axis.
