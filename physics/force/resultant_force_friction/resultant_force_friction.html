Answer 2 — option C

Starts at 20 N. Friction at 15 N applied is 15 N — it adjusts to match the push.

CORRECT
C · Starts at 20 N · friction at 15 N = 15 N
1
Normal force
N = m·g = 5 × 10 = 50 N
2
Maximum friction
f_max = μ·N = 0.40 × 50 = 20 N → block just starts to move when F > 20 N
3
At F = 15 N (below threshold)
Friction adjusts to exactly cancel the push: f = 15 N. Block stays still.
4
Net force on the block
F_net = 15 − 15 = 0 N → no acceleration

Question 3 — angled pull reduces friction

When the force is angled upward, what happens to the normal force?

QUESTION

A 10 N force is applied to a 2 kg block at 30° above the horizontal (so the force has both a forward pull and an upward lift). The coefficient of friction between block and floor is μ = 0.50. Does the block move? (Take g = 10 m/s².)

SECTION 21 / 21

Answer 3 — option C

Block moves. Lift from F sin θ reduces normal force → friction max is only 7.5 N.

CORRECT
C · Yes — block moves
1
Horizontal component (the push)
F cos θ = 10 × cos 30° ≈ 8.7 N
2
Vertical component (the lift)
F sin θ = 10 × sin 30° = 5 N (upward)
3
New normal force
N = mg − F sin θ = (2 × 10) − 5 = 15 N (not 20 N!)
4
Maximum friction with new N
f_max = μ · N = 0.50 × 15 = 7.5 N
5
Compare push vs friction
8.7 N (push) > 7.5 N (friction) → block moves
